    1 Overview

    C0 closes physically and financially under the fixed $2,000 cap. It is a 4-lift twin-boom quadplane: a 2.4 m NACA 2415 wing ( m², chord mm), four SunnySky X4110S lift motors on 17-in props and a fifth on a 14×8.5 pusher, 6061 aluminium booms and spar on a taped XPS foam wing, and ArduPilot QuadPlane on a CoreWing F405 Wing board. It hovers on W (thrust-to-weight ), cruises at 20 m/s on W, and has min of cruise on one 6S 6500 mAh pack after the hover allowance and 20 % reserve.

    Decisions needed from the client

    Which legal path and which flying field? Flight testing can't be scheduled until this is settled.
    The campus policy permits "University of California courses (including senior projects)", which covers the capstone. Site limits:
    • Richmond Field Station: 400 ft AGL
    • Clark Kerr track: 200 ft AGL
    • La Loma field: below 20 ft, inside the fences
    • All sites: 6 a.m. to sunset; no flying on game or graduation days
    Each flight needs a request filed ≥ 7 business days ahead and ≥ $1M liability cover per pilot. A transition needs ≥ 30 m AGL and 50–100 m of straight flight. That rules out La Loma, limits Clark Kerr to hover and low passes, and leaves Richmond Field Station as the only campus site for transitions.
    Recommended: by 2026-11-06, request Richmond Field Station from UCPD/Risk Services. Confirm the capstone's institutional sponsorship, which the § 350 educational path implies. Each pilot holds TRUST and AMA Full ($89, outside the cap). Alternative: one member passes Part 107 ($175) and the team flies at an off-campus field.

    Accept no hover motor-out tolerance (R14)?
    C0 cannot hold attitude after losing a lift motor in hover. The 8-lift (C1) keeps T/W after a motor-out, but its spend is , which is over the cap.
    Recommended: accept for C0. Keep hovers under 5 m AGL and under 60 s each, and qualify every motor on the thrust stand first. Alternative: none fits under $2,000.

    R14; analysis/results.json · §3

    How do the stopped lift props sit in cruise?
    BLHeli_S ESCs cannot stop a prop at a set angle, so a 2-blade prop stops wherever it lands. Folding props add $14–16 per station.
    Recommended: run the car-roof test (§3 M1) on one stopped unit at 20 m/s. If the prop windmills or sits crosswise, print a stop pawl. Alternative: Eolo folding 17×6.2, $49.99 a pair. Two pairs cost $99.98, about $110 with tax, and that exceeds the reserve.

    docs/research/budget-lift.md caveat 4; Props table (folding) · §3

    MTOW lands at kg, under the 8–15 kg working target.
    The 8–15 kg range was a working target, not a hard limit; the hard cap is 25 kg. The cost cap and the short mission drive mass down. A smaller aircraft is cheaper, easier to carry (R5) and safer to test.
    Recommended: accept, and record it in the design basis as pending team sign-off. Alternative: add payload or ballast to reach 8 kg. That needs more battery, motor and money and buys nothing.

    What changed from v0.2

    v0.2 cost and failed the cap. v0.3 kept the architecture and replaced every high-cost part; the v0.3.1 audit then drove seven design changes.

    v0.3.1 (same day): a six-reviewer project audit (§12) confirmed 79 findings. The design changes it drove:

    • booms moved outboard so the pusher disc clears the rear lift discs (it overlapped by 30 mm)
    • joiner lengthened to 0.76 m, for 0.30 m of engagement per side
    • wing thickened to NACA 2415, with the spar at 30 % chord, for foam cover over the socket
    • fifth servo for twin rudders
    • motor mounts moved to PA6-CF
    • previously missing wiring, glue, battery straps and a hot-wire allowance added
    • transition speed raised to 16 m/s (R11)

    2 CAD model

    The SOLIDWORKS model is generated from the sizing outputs by cad/build.ps1. Re-running the sizing and the build regenerates every part, so the CAD can't drift from the numbers. It has — parts in a —-component assembly:

    • NACA 2415 wing panels with 26 % chord ailerons and a spar socket bored at 30 % chord
    • a foam wing saddle with printed root plates that carries the root aerofoil across the pod over the joiner, held by one M5 screw per side
    • NACA 0009 tailplane seated on the booms, with an elevator fitted between lofted, tapered fins
    • turned motors, pusher spinner and pitot
    • tapered two-blade props, shown parked fore-aft (notional: BLHeli_S ESCs cannot park them)
    • printed boom clamps whose tops meet the wing surface at each station
    • PA6-CF motor mounts
    • gear clamps with legs and feet

    params.mjs checks the pusher-to-lift-disc clearance, currently mm (minimum 20 mm). Parts are placed by coordinates, not mates, and SOLIDWORKS interference detection has not been run, so interfaces are visual (—).

      4 Mass & power

      MTOW is the output of an energy-closed loop. Mission energy sets battery mass, mass sets wing area and hover power, and those feed back into the energy. With a continuous battery the loop closes at kg, needing Wh. The real pack chosen is the cheapest one that keeps ≥ 11.5 min of cruise, a 15 % design margin est over R10, on top of its 20 % energy reserve. It is the ( Wh), giving MTOW kg.

      What the team must measure

      1. M1
        Lift thrust curve on our pack. DIY stand (20 kg load cell + HX711 + Nano, calibrated with water jugs), watt meter in line, five throttle steps. Max under 2.4 kgf fails R13 (4 × 2.4 kgf / kg ≈ 1.6).
      2. M2
        Motor heat in a mission-length hover. Hold g for 90 s, read the can with the IR thermometer every 15 s, and let it cool below 45 °C before the next run. A mission has one hover of about 80 s at each end, with about 10 min of cruise cooling between. A can over 80 °C est after one 90 s run fails R9 hover duty (the lumped model predicts °C). Never fly back-to-back hovers: three 90 s runs with 30 s rests reach about °C in the model. The PA6-CF mounts tolerate the base temperature; PETG mounts would not (HDT about 68 °C).
      3. M3
        Pusher current and static thrust on 14×8.5EP. Thrust stand + watt meter. Static thrust under 1.5 kgf at 60 % throttle, or a peak over 40 A, means dropping to a 13×8 prop.
      4. M4
        Pack sag at hover current. Watt meter on all four lift motors at hover throttle, tied down. Also run a 3 s full-throttle burst on all four lift motors. Below 3.4 V/cell est at hover current, or below 3.2 V/cell in the burst, fails R13 margin. Move to the 8000 mAh pack (+$61, which exceeds the reserve).

      6 Structures

      The 6061-T6 wing joiner is the critical member. At ultimate load (4.5 g) the hand calc on the true 0.875 in joiner gives MPa, a reserve factor of on yield. That figure governs. The FEA models the joiner bonded inside the socket (OD = socket ID) and gives MPa on a 2 mm mesh, so it confirms the load path under that idealisation. Booms are strength-rich: MPa on a 1.5 mm mesh, with mm tip deflection at full thrust. Landing gear needs a compliant element with about 100 mm of travel if it behaves like a spring.

      Landing loads

      Energy balance, drop h = 0.30 m est. Bare aluminium legs act as springs, so read the right-hand column. A foam or TPU foot with about 100 mm of travel keeps n ≤ 8.

      Joints and materials

      • Wing–fuselage: 0.6 m aluminium joiner through the pod into the sockets, plus one M5 bolt per half at the trailing-edge rib for incidence and anti-rotation est.
      • Boom–wing: two printed PETG clamps per boom, seated on the wing lower surface (CAD §2), plus one M4 through-bolt per clamp to stop the boom rotating under differential thrust est. Typical PETG HDT is 68 °C (Prusament datasheet); the purchased ELEGOO PETG has no published HDT (—).
      • Motor mounts: printed PA6-CF20 saddles (HDT 173 °C, Polymaker datasheet). The lumped thermal model puts the motor near °C after a 90 s hover, too close to PETG's limit.
      • Wing: hot-wire FOAMULAR XPS core with packing-tape skin. σ_wing = kg/m² by F11. Tape mass per area is not published, so it is estimated est (M4).

      What the team must measure

      1. M1
        Wing proof load. Invert the wing on pod-width supports and sandbag it to limit load (3.0 × W), hold 30 s. Any permanent set in the joiner, or tip deflection over 10 % of semi-span, fails the wing for flight.
      2. M2
        Boom natural frequency. Tap the motor and record with a phone spectrum app. Within ±15 % of the hover rotor frequency (or twice it), add a strut before flight.
      3. M3
        Gear stroke. Drop the empty airframe 0.3 m onto grass; read peak g from the flight-controller log. Over 8 g, fit softer feet before the hover test.
      4. M4

      12 Simulation & verification

      Every SOLIDWORKS Simulation result agrees with its hand calculation within 15 %, and every margin passes. Each FEA model uses the same idealisation as its hand calc (rigid root, bonded joiner), so the FEA confirms the hand calcs rather than independently validating them. Clamp compliance would lower the boom frequency. The studies are FEA of the boom and the spar/joiner, a boom modal study and a lift-motor thermal study. A vortex-lattice model confirms the neutral point and the lift slope. An independent Python check re-derives every sizing formula and the BOM. Aerodynamic CFD was not run because SOLIDWORKS Flow Simulation would not load on this licence.

      Motor heat: steady FEA and 90 s hover

      Heat into each lift motor is estimated at 20 % of the hover input ( W) est, with prop-wash convection at 50 W/m²K est and 35 °C ambient. Steady-state FEA gives °C, a bulk-temperature estimate for unlimited hover. Hover lasts 60–90 s, so the time response decides. The lumped estimate for a 90 s hover is °C (band °C for c = 385–500 J/kgK; time constant s). Three back-to-back 90 s runs with 30 s rests would reach about °C, so T3 cools the motor to 45 °C between runs.
